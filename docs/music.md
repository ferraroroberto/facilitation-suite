# Music

An item can carry **music**: a file, or a Spotify playlist, album, artist or track — played on this PC's own audio output (the app plays it itself, so it never depends on a browser tab; route the PC's audio into Zoom/OBS as you already do). In the Plan tab, the item's **Music** row: switch it on, pick **File** or **Spotify** — **Choose file…** (mp3, wav, ogg or flac — m4a/AAC is not decoded; convert it first) or paste the Spotify link (Share → Copy link) — then its volume, fade-in and fade-out seconds, **Starts**, **When the timer ends** (only on an item with a timer) and **When leaving the item**, and **loop** (start over at the end). The file is **copied into the session folder** as `audio/<name>` (the way the roster is copied), so the session stays self-contained and *Files offline* covers it; a different file with the same name becomes `<name>-2`. In `session.yaml`:

```yaml
- kind: slide
  slide_id: 105
  timer: {seconds: 300, start: manual}
  music:
    path: audio/warm-up.mp3   # session-relative
    volume: 70                # 0–100
    fade_in_s: 3
    fade_out_s: 3
    loop: true
    start: with_timer         # with_timer | on_enter | manual
    on_leave: fade_out        # fade_out | keep_playing
    on_timer_end: fade_out    # fade_out | keep_playing (at 00:00; a reset always stops it)
- kind: break
  music: {source: spotify, uri: "https://open.spotify.com/playlist/…", start: on_enter}
```

**With the timer:** starting or resuming the item's timer fades the music in and plays it; pausing the timer fades it out and pauses it; **Reset** fades it out and stops it, and so does the timer reaching 00:00 — unless the item's **When the timer ends** is *Keep playing until I stop it* (then the music carries on past 00:00, still the item's, until you press **Stop** in the presenter, reset the timer or leave the item per **When leaving the item**; a restart brings it back too). `on_enter` plays when the item comes on stage (its timer still pauses and stops it); `manual` plays only from the presenter. Leaving the item fades it out and stops it, unless it is set to **keep playing** (then its own timer, still running, stops it at 00:00 — unless that item keeps playing at the end). A file that ends by itself just stops, or starts over with loop on.

**By hand:** the presenter's **Music** card (shown when the session has music, or Spotify is set up) plays any of the session's tracks — every item's, plus any other file in `audio/` — or a pasted Spotify link, with the item's own music picked when it comes on stage: **Play / Pause / Resume**, **Stop** (fades out over the track's fade-out), **Fade out** (a slow 8-second wind-down, then stop), a volume slider and — while a Spotify playlist, album or artist plays — **Previous track** and **Next track** (the volume dips out and back in around the skip; Spotify's own rule makes previous restart a track that is already a few seconds in; a file is one track, so they stay disabled). The phone remote has the same play/pause, previous/next, stop and volume. The Stream Deck actions are `music_toggle` (with nothing playing: the item's music, else the last track), `music_next`, `music_prev`, `music_stop`, `music_fade_out` and `music_volume/<0–100>`.

**Which wins:** the latest command. An item's timer and leaving the item only act on music **that item started itself**: music you play by hand is never paused or stopped by a timer, and a timer start on an item with *with the timer* music replaces whatever plays (the same track already playing is taken over, not restarted). Pausing, resuming or changing the volume by hand keeps the item in charge; stopping ends it. The quiz's sound cues rank below both: they never interrupt an item's music or music played by hand (see [Quiz → Sound cues](quiz.md#sound-cues)).

The presenter's chip says *Music · <track>* (playing), *paused*, *idle* or *error* — with the reason, e.g. no audio output device (speakers or headset disconnected) or a file missing from `audio/`; a music problem never stops the deck. The readiness list's **Music files** check (only when the session plays files) says whether every track is in the folder, on this PC and playable; its **Spotify** check (only when the session plays Spotify) says whether the login works and the Spotify desktop app on this PC is visible. **After a restart** the music comes back with the session, near its position (the time the server was down counts as played, like the item's timer): an item's music resumes, fading in, only if that item is still on stage and its timer — if it started one — is running; with its timer paused it comes back paused, and resuming the timer resumes it where it was; with another item on stage or its timer reset or ended, it stays silent. Music played by hand resumes, fading in, if it was playing, or comes back paused. Quiz cues never come back. Spotify carries on from wherever the desktop app is when it still holds the session's music; if the account has moved on to something else, the session's music starts over instead. After you close the session (×), going live again starts silent. Music starts and stops are logged (`facilitation-suite.log`, *sound is coming out* once the device plays) and appended to `live/events.jsonl`.

## Spotify setup (once)

Spotify is driven through its official Web API, on the **Spotify desktop app of this PC** only — never a web player (a **Premium** account — Spotify allows playback control only for Premium). The app keeps its client id and login in `.env` (gitignored) — never in the repo or `config/config.json`. `.env.example` (committed) lists the keys; copy it to `.env` before starting.

1. Open the [Spotify developer dashboard](https://developer.spotify.com/dashboard), log in with the Premium account and **Create app**: any name and description, **Redirect URI** `http://127.0.0.1:8765/callback` (exactly — Spotify only accepts the loopback address, not `localhost`), API **Web API**. Save.
2. In the app's **Settings**, copy the **Client ID** into `.env` at the repo root: `SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID=<client id>`. (No client secret: the login uses PKCE.)
3. Open the Spotify desktop app on this PC, logged in to the same account. A web player (open.spotify.com in a tab) is never used, even when it is the only player open.
4. In the app, **Settings → Music → Connect Spotify** (on this PC): the browser asks you to allow the app, and the app writes `SPOTIFY_REFRESH_TOKEN` to `.env` — the card then shows the account's state, the device it plays on and the last check; it never shows the client id or the token. **Reconnect Spotify** replaces the login. The same login from a terminal: `& .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\spotify_login.py`, which also prints the account type and the devices Spotify sees (it ends with *The desktop app on this PC is ready*); another port there: `--port 8766` (and that redirect URI in the dashboard). No restart needed either way.
   A login made before the account-type permission was added (it printed *Account: unknown* or *not checked*) still plays, but **connect again once** so it can read the account type; the script then prints *Account: premium*.
5. Where it plays: the `Computer` device named like this PC; else any other desktop app Spotify lists as a computer (the active one first), whatever name it reports. A web player is never a target: Spotify keeps listing a closed tab, even as active, for a while, and every tab has the same name, so the app cannot tell a ghost from a live one. With the desktop app closed, Settings › Music says *Desktop app not open*, and the readiness list and a play on the presenter say *The Spotify desktop app is not open on this PC*. If Spotify shows the app under another name and several computers are listed, set `SPOTIFY_DEVICE_NAME=<name>` in `.env` to pin it.

   **Never a silent "Playing".** Spotify accepting a command is not proof of sound: after every play or resume the app reads Spotify's player state until it shows the desktop app playing with its position moving (up to 5 s, the volume held at 0 meanwhile); only then does the music count as playing. Otherwise the presenter shows *Spotify accepted the command but nothing is playing on <device>*, with what Spotify reports instead. A command Spotify still refuses with 403 "Restriction violated" is shown as *Command refused*, not a raw error.

Fades step Spotify's volume twice a second; a stop fades out, pauses (Spotify has no stop) and puts the app's volume back where it was.

**Real names.** A Spotify link is listed by its real name — a playlist's or artist's, an album's or track's as "name — artist" — in the presenter's picker and on the Music card, instead of "Spotify playlist 4HufCB". The name is asked of Spotify once, in the background, as soon as the account is connected, and kept in `data/spotify-names.json` on this PC (a cache: delete it and the names are fetched again); until it arrives, when the account is not connected, or when Spotify will not say (a private playlist your login cannot read — retried every 5 minutes), the label is the kind and the first six characters of the id.

**Crossfade between tracks.** The app cannot set it: Spotify's Web API has no crossfade endpoint or parameter. Its Player reference (developer.spotify.com → Web API → Player, checked 2026-10-03) lists exactly Get Playback State, Transfer Playback, Get Available Devices, Get Currently Playing Track, Start/Resume Playback, Pause Playback, Skip To Next, Skip To Previous, Seek To Position, Set Repeat Mode, Set Playback Volume, Toggle Playback Shuffle, Get Recently Played Tracks, Get the User's Queue and Add Item to Playback Queue, and the community threads asking for one are still open. Crossfade is a setting of the Spotify desktop app itself. To blend the tracks of a playlist, turn it on there: **Settings → Playback → Crossfade songs**, with the slider at the seconds you want. The app's own fades stay as they are — the music fades in when it starts and out when it stops, and moving to the next or previous track (above) dips the volume out and back in around the skip — so between items, and when you skip, the volume ramp is the fade; a true overlap of two tracks is only ever the desktop setting. The chip and the readiness list keep the failures apart: **not set up** (no client id or login in `.env`, or a wrong client id), **login expired** (revoked or expired — Settings → Music → Reconnect Spotify), **desktop app not open** (open the Spotify desktop app on this PC), **not playing** (Spotify accepted a play but the app is not playing it), **command refused** (403 "Restriction violated"), **Premium needed**, and **unknown** when Spotify could not be reached or is rate-limiting — never counted as ready.
